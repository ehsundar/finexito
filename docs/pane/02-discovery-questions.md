# Pane — Questions for the Client

He does not know what an app can do, so do not ask him what he wants. Ask him
how the shop works, and derive the app from the answers. Ideally do this
standing in the shop for an hour, watching two or three real customers.

## Watch before you ask

- Where does an order get written down? Ask to photograph a week of slips.
- What is on the bench next to the cutting table?
- How many pieces are stacked waiting to be collected, and how old are they?
- How big is the off-cut pile?

## The order

1. Someone walks in wanting a mirror cut. Talk me through it, from hello to them
   leaving. What do you write down?
2. How do you know what to make tomorrow morning?
3. Has a job ever been forgotten or cut wrong? What happened?
4. How do customers know it is ready?

## Price

5. How do you work out the price for a 90 × 60 mirror? Say it out loud.
6. What do you charge extra for — bevelling, holes, polished edges, delivery?
7. Is there a minimum charge for a small piece?
8. Do some customers pay a different price than others?
9. When the supplier raises the glass price, how do your prices change?

## Materials

10. What do you keep in stock, and how do you know when to reorder?
11. What happens to the off-cuts? Do you ever hunt through them for a piece?
12. Roughly what proportion of a sheet becomes scrap?

## Money

13. Do you take a deposit? Always, or only for big jobs?
14. Right now, how much money is owed to you? (If he cannot answer quickly,
    that is the pitch.)

## People and kit

15. Who else works here, and what do they need to see? What must they *not* see?
16. What phone do you use? Is there a tablet or computer in the shop? Wifi?
17. Do you use WhatsApp with customers? Do you send SMS?
18. Which customers come back again and again? Builders, carpenters, offices?
    Would they look something up themselves if they could, or would they still
    just ring you?

## The close

19. If you could stop doing one thing tomorrow, what would it be?
20. What do you spend most of your day on that is not cutting glass?

## Then show him, do not tell him

Come back with a clickable version of the order form pre-filled with a job from
his own slips, and the off-cut search. Two screens. Let him press the buttons.
Feature lists do not land with someone who has never used a business app; a
screen with his own customer's name on it does.
